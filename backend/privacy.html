Compass — Privacy Policy

Effective 2 October 2026 · Applies to the Compass Chrome extension

Compass helps you understand and find your way around the website you are viewing: you ask a question in the side panel, Compass explains the answer using that website's pages, and takes you to the right page and section. This policy explains what data that involves.

Summary

  • Compass only reads the tab you are looking at, and only while its side panel is open.
  • When you open the panel on a website, Compass sends that website's address and its navigation links to our server, which then reads the website's public pages so Compass can search all of them.
  • When you ask, Compass sends your question, this page's headings and the text under them, and your last two questions in this conversation. They are not stored.
  • No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no selling of data.

What Compass reads on your computer

When the side panel is open, Compass reads the page in the active tab: its address, its title, its headings (h1–h3), where each heading sits on the page, up to about 1,200 characters of text under each heading, and the links in the site's navigation. To find links hidden in dropdown menus, Compass briefly opens the navigation's menus and closes them again. This happens inside your browser.

Compass cannot read Chrome's own pages (such as Settings or the Chrome Web Store).

What is sent to our server, and when

WhenDataWhy
You open the panel on a websiteThe page address (without query strings or # fragments) and the links in the site's navigation menus: their addresses and labelsSo our server can read the website's public pages, including ones only reachable through dropdown menus
You ask a questionYour question; the page address and title; the page's headings and the text under each one; your last two questions and Compass's answers in this conversationTo find and explain the answer, and to understand follow-up questions

How our server reads a website: it downloads the website's public pages (at most 80, following robots.txt), the same way a search engine does. It never uses your cookies or logins, so it can't see anything that needs you to be signed in. The resulting index contains only public content, and is shared by everyone who uses Compass on that website.

How an answer is made: the server finds the sections that best match your question, then sends your question, up to six of those sections and your recent questions to Groq, our AI model provider, which writes the explanation.

Compass does not send the full page, form contents, passwords, cookies, or your browsing history.

What is stored

  • On our server: your questions and the content read from your browser are not written to disk. A short-lived in-memory cache keeps the numeric embeddings of recently asked-about pages (not the text) so follow-up questions are faster; it is cleared whenever the server restarts. The index of a website's public pages (made by our server, as described above) is kept so the next visitor doesn't have to wait. Our hosting provider (Render) keeps standard request logs, such as IP address, time and the endpoint called, for operating the service.
  • In your browser: your last few questions and the site they were asked on, shown under "Recent". They are stored with Chrome's extension storage on your device only. You can clear them with the Clear button, or by removing the extension.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell or rent your data, or use it for advertising.
  • We don't use it to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
  • We don't build profiles of you or track you across sites.
  • Compass does not run any code downloaded from our server. Server replies are treated as data only.

The use of information received by Compass adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
